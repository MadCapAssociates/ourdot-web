OurDot vs Messenger Kids

Both are free, ad-free and parent-controlled. The difference is who the app is built around: a child's approved contact list, or the whole family in one shared space.

By the OurDot team · Updated October 24, 2026

In short

Messenger Kids is a mature, free messaging app for kids under 13 where parents approve each contact. OurDot is a whole-family app, for all ages, where kids share, message, play and learn inside one invite-only family, and adults other than guardians never chat with a child one-on-one.

What Messenger Kids does well

It's free, has no ads or in-app purchases, doesn't need a Facebook account for the child, gives parents a dashboard to approve and remove contacts, includes sleep mode, and lets parents download their child's data. It's been around since 2017, and many families already use it.

Where they differ

Messenger Kids is built around a child's contact list; OurDot is built around a family. In OurDot, grandparents, aunts and family friends talk with kids in shared family spaces with a guardian present, not one-on-one, and the same app grows with kids into their teens.

A note on the FTC

In May 2023, the FTC alleged that between late 2017 and mid-2019, flaws in Messenger Kids let children join group chats and calls with contacts their parents hadn't approved, and proposed changes to Meta's earlier privacy order. Meta disputes the proceedings, and as of February 2026 they were unresolved. These are allegations, not findings.
