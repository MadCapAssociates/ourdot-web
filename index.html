Why we built it

Kids are on apps built for adults, and designed to keep them scrolling.

We wanted the good parts, staying close to family and sharing everyday life, without the parts built to hook kids.

What's inside

Everything a family shares, in one calm place.

Grandparents, guardians, kids, aunts, uncles and close family friends. Nobody joins unless a guardian invites them.

Messages first

The app opens to your people, not a feed. Talk one-to-one, in family groups, by voice or on a video call.

A feed that ends

Newest first, then "You're all caught up." No algorithm, no public like counts. Hearts are private.

Play together

Our own family games: a daily word puzzle, logic puzzles, trivia about each other, drawing, a maze, a quest and more. No ads, purchases or streaks.

Learn

Three-minute lessons on being safe and kind online, plus a library of topics you choose. Every lesson shows its sources.

Plan cook-together nights, find a job for every helper by age, and share one shopping list. Never about weight, always about time together.

Guardians stay in charge

School-time and sleep-time locks, daily game time, approvals for who joins. The line to a guardian always stays open.

The science

Every feature starts with the research.

Where the evidence is strong, we say so. Where it's mixed, we say that too. Each choice links to where it came from.

A feed that ends

Newest first, then you're done. No endless scroll, no autoplay, no "For You" page.

Strong guidance

The Surgeon General names endless scrolling, autoplay and push notifications as features designed to maximize time on an app.

US Surgeon General's Advisory, May 2023

Private hearts, no like counts

Only the person who posted sees who sent a heart.

Strong guidance

Public "likes" are listed among engagement-driving features, and the American Psychological Association advises limiting social media use for comparing yourself to others.

Surgeon General, May 2023 · APA advisory via OPB, May 2023

Messages come first

The app opens to conversations with family, not a feed.

Modest evidence

A review of 141 studies found "active" use like messaging and posting linked to somewhat better wellbeing and social support. Most effects were small.

Godard & Holtzman, Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication, Dec 2023

Sleep mode

Guardians set it (8:30 pm to 7 am by default). Everything rests except the guardian line.

Strong evidence

Across 20 studies of 125,198 children, bedtime device use was linked to about twice the odds of too little sleep. The Surgeon General recommends no devices for at least an hour before bed.

Carter et al., JAMA Pediatrics, Dec 2016

School mode

Guardians choose it, to protect focus in class.

Honestly mixed

A study of 1,227 students in 30 English schools found phone limits cut phone use during school, but found no difference in wellbeing or total daily use. That's why it's the guardian's call.

Goodyear et al., Lancet Regional Health – Europe, Feb 2025

"Before you share" check

A gentle "Pause for a sec?" before anything unkind goes out. Kids practice checking their own words.

One helpful layer

In a test with more than 200,000 users, a prompt before posting possibly offensive replies led people to cancel 9% and revise 22% of them. It studied adults, so we treat it as one layer, not a fix.

Katsaros, Yang & Fratamico, ICWSM, 2022

The same open rules for every adult

Trusted adults see family posts and write notes, but never chat privately one-on-one with kids. Guardians can remove anyone instantly.

Follows how harm happens

In national data, 71% of children who were harmed knew the person; 18% were strangers. Being known or related isn't a safety signal on its own. No study tests this exact design.

Finkelhor, Hammer & Sedlak, OJJDP Bulletin, Aug 2008

More privacy as kids grow

Fuller guardian checks under 13, lighter at 13 to 15, optional at 16 and up.

Expert guidance

The APA recommends closer guardian involvement in early adolescence (about 10 to 14), then more privacy as kids build skills.

APA advisory via OPB, May 2023

Guardian-set game time

Each guardian picks daily game time per child. Lessons never count against it.

Expert guidance

Pediatricians don't set one number for every child. The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends each family make its own media plan.

AAP Family Media Plan

Cook-together nights, no streaks

Family Kitchen counts any shared meal, breakfast or video-call dinner. It never nags about missed nights.

Associations, not proof

Across 17 studies of more than 182,000 children, three or more shared meals a week were linked to healthier eating. Other research finds family relationships explain much of that, so we focus on connection, not counting.

Hammons & Fiese, Pediatrics, 2011 · Musick & Meier, Journal of Marriage and Family, 2012

How families join

Invite-only, from the very first person.

  1. A guardian sets up the family.

    They invite the people their kids already know and trust.

  2. Everyone signs in with a code.

    A 6-digit code comes by email. No password to remember.

  3. Kids join through their guardian.

    Use your own email with a + and their name, like you+leo@gmail.com. Codes always come to you, and we never store a child's own email.

  4. Tell us what you think.

    Tap the 💬 tab on any screen. Every note is read, and nothing changes until we've looked into it.

About
