What makes a social app safe for kids? A checklist

No app is risk-free. But the safest designs share a pattern, and it matches what pediatricians, psychologists and regulators ask for. Use this checklist on any app, including ours.

By the OurDot team · Updated October 24, 2026

The short answer

Look for an app that is private by default, keeps strangers from contacting kids, collects as little data as possible, shows no targeted ads, doesn't push kids to keep scrolling, and makes it easy to block, report and get help. These match what the AAP, APA, the UK Children's Code and the EU's guidelines ask platforms to do. None of these bodies certify apps.

We included the row where OurDot falls short on purpose. A checklist is only useful if it's honest.

Questions to ask about any app

  • Who can find my child, and who can message them?
  • Does the feed end? Is there autoplay?
  • Are there public likes, followers or streaks?
  • How does the app make money? If it's ads, what does it know about my child?
  • Can my child report something easily, and who reads it?
  • Will my child know what I can see?
